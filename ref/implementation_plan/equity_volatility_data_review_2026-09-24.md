# Equity volatility data review — 2026-09-24

Read-only audit of `D:/DBs/timeseries_analysis/history_coverage.db` (23,995,736,064 bytes; last modified 2026-09-20). No source rows, derived tables, splits, thresholds, or training settings were changed. Counts below are database observations, not claims about true market prices.

## Current pipeline

`data/data_fetching.py` downloads Yahoo OHLCV with `auto_adjust=True`. `data/roughness_analysis.py` retains 25-year complete-coverage equities and calculates unannualized daily reduced Garman–Klass variance: 0.5 ln(High/Low)² − (2 ln 2 − 1) ln(Close/Open)². It rejects nonfinite or nonpositive OHLC, inconsistent High/Low bounds, negative volume, and nonpositive variance. `models/training_blocks.py` then takes the next *retained observation* within each ticker as the target; log-volatility runs use 0.5 ln(variance) and ln(Close/Open) as inputs. The pre-2016/2016–2018/2019–2025 splits are chronological.

## Findings

1. **The retained panel has a much heavier upper tail than the liquid-stock reference sample.** There are 9,587,674 eligible raw rows across 1,525 tickers. The variance table retains 9,217,433 rows: 36,632 fail OHLCV validity checks and 333,609 yield exactly zero Garman–Klass variance. None of the valid rows has negative variance. Every zero row has Open = High = Low = Close; 189,937 report zero volume and 143,672 report positive volume. The pre-2016 training variance median is 0.00023599, 99.9th percentile 0.04973, and maximum 14.92673; 47 training values exceed 1. These are daily, unannualized variance units.

   For the paper's 2011-07-01 through 2021-06-30 calendar period, all retained equities have 3,733,755 rows, 99.9th-percentile variance 0.03864, 881 values above 0.1, and maximum 3.19843. Of the 93 ticker symbols printed in Zhang et al.'s Table 1, 81 match this database; those 81 have 203,643 rows, 99.9th percentile 0.00645, no value above 0.1, and maximum 0.03965 over the same dates. This is a **within-database, symbol-based diagnostic**, not a reproduction of the paper's source, sample, or realized-volatility estimator. Twelve paper symbols are absent from this 25-year coverage panel.

2. **Some extreme inputs deserve source verification.** POCI on 2010-06-17 has adjusted Open = High = Close ≈ 7.08, Low ≈ 0.03, Volume = 167, and Garman–Klass variance 14.92673. The surrounding retained raw prices are near 6–9. NEWT on 2008-09-29 has Low ≈ 0.014 against Open ≈ 0.861 and nearby prices around 0.6–0.9. The top 20 variance values recompute exactly from the stored adjusted OHLC using the project formula; the calculation is correct for these rows, but the underlying price prints need independent verification before they are called data errors or removed. In the training split outside the 81-symbol reference subset, 23,455 retained rows have zero reported volume, 172 have High/Low > 3, 18 have High/Low > 10, and 82 have |ln(Close/Open)| > 1. The 81-symbol subset has none of these four conditions in that split.

3. **The target horizon is not always one trading day.** Removing zero-variance days makes the next retained observation irregular. Of 5,416,755 training windows, 13,550 targets follow a gap longer than seven calendar days and 895 follow a gap longer than 30 days. The corresponding counts are 1,279 and 102 in validation, and 1,313 and 107 in test. The longest retained gap is 582 days (CAPS, 2021-11-18 to 2023-06-23). POCI has a 61-day gap from 2010-06-23 to 2010-08-23. The model is therefore trained on a mixture of horizons even though the research objective says one-step daily variance. The current code documents *next observation*, but the experiment description should not equate every target with the next trading day.

4. **The saved SiLU model reacts more sharply to some extreme input days, but the failed epoch's individual outputs were not saved.** On the first retained target after NEWT's 2008-09-29 extreme range, the best-epoch-15 SiLU checkpoint forecasts variance 3.3110 against actual 0.00986; the best-epoch-43 base checkpoint forecasts 0.08323 on the same target. After EHC's 2003-04-09 extreme range, the forecasts are 0.52568 and 0.09710 against actual 0.00840. These are descriptive diagnostics using different best checkpoints. They do not prove that any specific row caused the epoch-18 training MSE overflow.

## Reference comparison and limits

[Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.08962) study 93 of the top 100 most liquid S&P 500 constituents with Nasdaq ITCH minute mid-price data, use log realized volatility from summed squared minute returns, winsorize each stock's return/volatility at 0.5% and 99.5%, and normalize inputs within each rolling estimation. Their LSTM uses standard tanh cell operations and an ensemble. This differs in universe, data vendor, proxy, outlier handling, input scaling, and architecture from this project. Their winsorization procedure should not be copied across this project's validation/test periods without checking for look-ahead; any learned bounds here must be fitted on training data only.

The broader [global-neural-network study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.02072) uses 11,771 stocks across 10 exchanges, so broad panels are not inherently invalid. It models daily close-to-close returns with a likelihood objective and a standard tanh LSTM, rather than this project's adjusted-OHLC Garman–Klass proxy and variance QLIKE. Its reported losses cannot be directly compared with this project's QLIKE. [S&P DJI's methodology](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/methodology/article/sp-us-indices-methodology/) defines the S&P 500 as a selected large-cap index; NYSE and Nasdaq are trading venues, not comparable volatility indexes. This database lacks historical exchange and S&P membership fields, so exchange-specific or true historical-constituent volatility differences were not estimated.

## Next checks before retraining

- Verify a small set of suspicious OHLC rows against an independent historical source, starting with POCI 2010-06-17 and NEWT 2008-09-29. Preserve the original rows and record source discrepancies.
- Decide whether the target is the next trading day or the next valid variance observation. If daily, explicitly handle dropped zero-variance days and long gaps, then rerun identical observation filters for every model and baseline.
- Compare a training-only, liquid-stock sensitivity subset and a training-fitted outlier treatment against the unchanged full panel. Do not infer a universal cleaning threshold from the validation or test period.
- Log the ticker, date, input-range summary, and maximum log-variance forecast on any future scoring failure. The failed epoch-18 individual predictions were not retained, so the exact cause of that overflow remains unobserved.
